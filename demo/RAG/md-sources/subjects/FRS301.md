### Description - FRS301
This course addresses the comprehension and application of Digital Forensic Investigations. Students will evaluate and synthesize technical and legal issues in relation to digital evidence. Students will apply various skills and techniques, combined with numerous investigative software tools to analyze seized electronic media. Students subject to background investigation prior to admittance.  Assessment scheme:  1) On-going Assessment - 01 Midterm Progress Test: 20% - Participation in Discussions: 10% - 14 Lab-Projects: 40% 2) 01 Final Exam: 30% 3) Final Result 100% Completion Criteria: 1) Every on-going assessment component >0 2) Average Lab Score >= 4 3) Final Exam Score >=4 & Final Result >=5

## Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) - FRS301

- **CLO1**: Defining Digital Forensics and examining how it’s being used.
- **CLO2**: Understanding the underlying technology powering cell phones and GPS units, as well as the potential evidence they could contain.
- **CLO3**: Understanding how computers create and store digital information is a perquisite for the study of digital forensics.
- **CLO4**: Examining digital forensic environment, hardware and software, and standards used on a regular basis, to accredit labs and validate tools.
- **CLO5**: Collecting digital evidence, establishing a chain of custody, and understanding its major role when admitted into court.
- **CLO6**: Understanding many of the common Windows artifacts and how they are created.
- **CLO7**: Examining tools and techniques used to hide or destroy data with the aim to recover digital evidence, deleted files, and the like.
- **CLO8**: Explaining the legal aspects of digital forensics in searching.
- **CLO9**: Understanding social networks, e-mail, chat logs, and Internet history representing some of the best evidence.
- **CLO10**: Understanding how networks are attacked and what role digital forensics plays in not only the response, but in how perpetrators can be traced.
