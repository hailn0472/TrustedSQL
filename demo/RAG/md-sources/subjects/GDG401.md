# GDG401 - 3D Animation


## Course Information - GDG401

- **Credits**: 6
- **Degree Level**: Undergraduate
- **Time Allocation**: 60 slots (1 slot = 90')
- **Pre-Requisite**: DTG201 and GDS301
- **Min Avg Mark To Pass**: 5
- **Scoring Scale**: 10

### Description - GDG401
This course will prepare you for further study in higher level topics of 3D animation. In this course, you will learn all the tools to create your own animation and execute the entire visual production.

### Student Tasks - GDG401
Attendance: The course content and assigments are closely tied together; excessive absences will severely and negatively affect the learning process. Any student who misses 20% classes will fail the course.  Assigments: You are given weekly assignments that are completed during the week. Assignments are evaluated against a set of technical criteria as well as on aesthetic quality. Assigments will account for 50 % of your grade. Late assignments will result in an automatic lowering of the grade. Attendance at each class and lab is expected. Missing classes or late arrival will result in a lowered grade.  Final project: There will be an animated film, duration about 2 minutes, that will account for 50 % of your grade. The film will be determined and assigned at the end of this course.  Plagiarism: is claiming the work someone else did, as work you did. DO NOT DO IT!!!! If you claim that you created the work someone else created, you will not pass the course.

**Session 22**: The Twelve Basic Priciples of Animation (part 1) The purpose of animation is to serve the story e to capture images of life, and in the process communicate to others, even if it just means giving them a thrill ride. To this end, you need to make your animation believable. This doesn’t just mean realism. There are cues that pull people into the action. Animators at Walt Disney Studio wanted to create a kind of animation that felt real and let the audience respond to the character and story. As they observed and practiced what was at the time a new art, they developed a set of guidelines for animating. Two of these pioneers, Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston, set them out in 1981 in their book The Illusion of Life: Disney Animation. These guidelines have become standard for the animation industry.  Learning Outcomes: In this lesson, you will learn: 1 - Squash and Stretch 2 - Anticipation 3 - Staging 4 - Straight-Ahead Action and Pose to Pose 5 - Follow Through and Overlapping Action 6 - Arcs

**Session 40**: Modeling and texturing Modeling and texturing are two of the primary jobs within the field of 3D animation and are closely tied to one another. Modeling is the act of creating a 3D object. Texturing is applying the color and surface properties to the model. Every industry that uses 3D animation must start with the creation of a model, which then serves as the foundation for all the other steps in the production pipeline. Texturing is the stage after a model is created, and the application of the texture depends on the geometry type used to create the model.
