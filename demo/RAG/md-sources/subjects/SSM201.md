### Description - SSM201
This course provides students with personal and interpersonal skills needed for effective leadership and supervision, especially it focuses on creative thinking and problem solving. This course is skill-based approach, therefore requires a lot of practical exercises. After taking this class, the students should be able: 1. To increase your understanding of core behavioral principles upon which important management skills are based 2. To help foster improved competency in several critical management skills 3. To help prepare you to transfer this learning and improvement to real-life, out-of-class settings 4. To develop individual personal ability and skills to work collaboratively

### Student Tasks - SSM201

## Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) - SSM201

- **CLO1**: Demonstrate critical-thinking and problem solving skills
- **CLO2**: Demonstrate effective written and oral communication
- **CLO3**: Demonstrate the ability to recognize when change is needed, adapt to change as it occurs, and lead change

## Assessment - SSM201

### Essay Test
- Weight: 10.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- Question Type: OP1: essays, exercises, or questions ; OP2: (For Constructivism Approach only): Follow lecturer's proposal

### Group assignment
- Weight: 20.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- Question Type: OP1: The purpose of this assignment is to give students a work-team experience in developing management skills. To arrive at a realistic problem, each group should interview one manager or alternatively carrying out surveys on management problems. The group must assume the role of a consulting firm called in to solve that identified specific problem in the chosen organization. Groups should also use descriptions of current management practices reported in business periodicals. All projects must be approved by the instructor before being started. Each group will make an in-class PowerPoint presentation of about 30 minutes, in PowerPoint on a real or fictitious organizational problem. (The problem must be related to a chapter topic. OP2: (For Constructivism Approach only): Follow lecturer's proposal
- Note: OP1: All assignments should be typed and double-spaced with 11-point font, and must be submitted in both soft and hard copy. A deduction of 10% of the assignment mark will be applied for each day of late submission
The report and presentation should roughly follow this format: (1) nature and background of firm; (2) description of their problem; (3) proposed remedy (including a convincing justification based on successes in other firms or accepted management principles); (4) plan for implementation; and (5) question-and-answer period.
Evaluation will be based on both presentation and written report. Specific contacts of interview including phone number, addresses, website must be submitted

OP2: (For Constructivism Approach only): Follow lecturer's proposal

### Individual assignment
- Weight: 10.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- Question Type: OP1: Students will be asked to complete a written individual assignment. This will give students opportunities to show their critical and analytical thinking about management skills (good or bad or both) of any manager (a famous manager in the world/ from a movie/ or a manager that students interview in their real life). Based on the theories studied, students will provide their critical analysis, evaluation, and recommendations. Details on the format, length and other expectations will be provided by lecturer in class. OP2: (For Constructivism Approach only): Follow lecturer's proposal
- Note: Any plagiarism will be resulted in a failure of assignment

OP2: (For Constructivism Approach only): Follow lecturer's proposal

### Skill Application Exercises
- Weight: 20.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0

Your role either individually or in group to demonstrate your application of management skills will be assigned randomly prior to the seminar at least 2 days via your private e-mail and will be assessed by the lecturer, the debate group and the audience. Please note that the skill based exercises are ongoing and continuous in every session, which implicates that the number of times you will be assigned to fulfill your tasks either individually or in-group are various as well.
