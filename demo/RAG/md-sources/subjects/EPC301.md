### Description - EPC301
Objectives: The course is designed to help undergraduate students to grasp the key concepts and practices of persuasive communication today. Through real-life situations as well as authentic materials, students will be able to develop their communication and persuasion skills.  Description EPC301 provides students with a comprehensive yet accessible overview of the study and practice of persuasive communication. It also demonstrates how theories and research illuminate and adapt to the present digital era, with continued attention to ethical implications and today’s big topics. The course includes up-to-date definitions of key terms in the field as they relate to present-day practice; an integration of persuasion theories and the ubiquity of online influence; new examples and cases to illustrate persuasive communication’s approach to health campaigns, attitudes, communicator appeals, dissonance, and ethics; and a thorough reflection of the most current scholarship in the field.  Teaching Methods: The teaching & learning process includes a variety of methods: From teachers’ side: lectures, guided learning, feedback and corrections; From students’ side: discussions, presentations, research, writing and self-study.  Assessment scheme: 1) On-going assessment - Assignments 02*20% 40% - Presentation 01*20% 20% 2) Final examination (Project): 40% 3) Final Result 100% 4) Completion Criteria: a) On-going assessment >0, Each component >0 b) Final examination: >=4 c) Final Result >=5

## Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) - EPC301

- **CLO1**: Understand key concepts related to persuation LO1.1 understand the constancies and changes of persuasion LO1.2 recognize the features of persuasion LO1.3 identify the differences between persuasion and coercion LO1.4 understand persuasive communication effects LO1.5 identify the requirements of an ethical persuader
- **CLO2**: Recognize the emotional message appeals: fear and guilt
- **CLO3**: Understand the definition and structure of attitudes LO2.1 understand the concept and characteristics of attitudes LO2.2 identify the structure of attitudes LO2.3 recognize the relationship between attitude structure and persuasion
- **CLO4**: Recognize the power of strong attitudes LO3.1 understand the concept of strong attitudes LO3.2 recognize the effects of strong attitudes
- **CLO5**: Identify the functions and consequences of attitudes LO4.1 understand the functions of attitudes LO4.2 identify the relationship between attitudes and behavior LO4.3 judge consistencies: gray areas and ethical issues
- **CLO6**: Apply different methods to measure attitudes LO5.1 use questionnaires to measure attitudes LO5.2 use indirect methods to measure attitudes LO5.3 understand pitfalls in attitude measurement LO5.4 ask good questions regarding attitude measurement
- **CLO7**: Process persuasive communications LO6.1 understand historical foundations and ELM principles LO6.2 recognize peripheral processing and central processing
- **CLO8**: Understand the communicator
- **CLO9**: Understand the fundamentals of the message LO9.1 identify the message structure LO9.2 identify the message content LO9.3 use effective language in the message
- **CLO10**: Run a communicative campaign and develop flexibility and respect in team work
