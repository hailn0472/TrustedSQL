### Description - FBM201
This course has been designed to achieve the following objectives: To study the skills and science of food and beverage management. The systems approach to food and beverage (F&B) management is presented and includes planning and control of F&B operations. Management operation systems are also presented and integrated with F&B delivery systems for a complete F&B overview. Description: Managing food and beverage is essential for the hospitality industry. This course addresses the key concepts of the food and beverage industry and the management aspects of the trade

## Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) - FBM201

- **CLO1**: Develop general knowledge on the origins and development of food service in hotels, restaurants, and institutions.
- **CLO2**: Practice current managerial techniques and trends to support the goals of a restaurant.
- **CLO3**: Use decision-making skills and abilities in a variety of managerial, production, and service positions that are typical of the food service industry.
- **CLO4**: Interpret financial data as it relates to an operation’s goals.
- **CLO5**: Describe the economic impact of the F&B industry.
- **CLO6**: Distinguish between commercial and institutional food service facilities.
- **CLO7**: Identify trends likely to affect food service in the coming years.
- **CLO8**: Identify a variety of managerial, production, and service positions that are typical of the food service industry and describe the roles these positions play in providing food service.
- **CLO9**: Describe managerial responsibilities as they relate to food service functions including menu planning, purchasing, storing, preparation, and recipe development.
- **CLO10**: Define the steps involved in menu planning and menu design.
- **CLO11**: Define the importance and role of Food and Beverage marketing.
- **CLO12**: Identify the major functions and basic principles of Food and Beverage production.

- Question Type: Option 1: Each student shoud make at least 1 individual presentation, case analysis during the course in front of the class. Topics will be varied based on the topics in the textbook.
Option 2 (For Constructivism Approach only): Follow lecturer's proposal
- Note: Option 1: Participation grade will be given based on students' preparation, and quantity as well as quality of participation in classroom; quality of participation is demonstrated through asking relevant questions, making statements that add to and facilitate class discussion, and builds upon others’ comments. ### Group assignment
- Weight: 25.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- Question Type: Option 1: Four or five students per group.
Groups and groups ideas are determined on the third session of the course. Work will be break down into stages and examined during the course. Final work to be submitted prior to the presentation of the topic.
Option 2 (For Constructivism Approach only): Follow lecturer's proposal
- Note: Option 1: All assignments should be typed and double-spaced with 11-point font, and must be submitted in both soft and hard copy. A deduction of 10% of the assignment mark will be applied for each day of late submission
