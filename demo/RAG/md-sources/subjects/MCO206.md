### Description - MCO206
This course presents a general overview of the key issues and theories in the area of intercultural communication. It critically examines the relationship between culture, identity and communication. Using a wide variety of authentic materials, tasks and media, this course adopts a thematic approach to explore how people communicate both within and across various cultural settings.

## Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) - MCO206

- **CLO1**: Demonstrate specialised and integrated knowledge of intercultural communication theories, terms, context and power
- **CLO2**: Enhance the ability to select and use the appropriate style and means of communication for distinctive cultural groups
- **CLO3**: Enhance the ablity to understand people based on their cutural identity
- **CLO4**: Enhance the ability to engage sensitively and confidently with individuals, groups, and communities in diverse local, national and cultural contexts
- **CLO5**: Develop a critical attitude towards culture identity
- **CLO6**: Develop an attitude of open-mindedness and self-critical reflection with a view to self-improvement
- **CLO7**: Enhance the ability to predict the consequences of ethics in intercultural communication

### Personal Reflection Project
- Weight: 40.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- CLO: LO1, LO2, LO3, LO6, LO7
- Question Type: Option 1: Individual Project Option 2: (For Constructivism Approach only): Follow lecturer's proposal
- Note: Option 1:
Task Description:
Find your culture (subculture) and embrace your own culture. This assessment provides you with an opportunity to reflect on your culture as an "insider". You are required to apply at least one of the theories or conceptual models you have learned in the course. This assessment provides you with an opportunity to identify who you are, which culture you belong to and the characteristics of your culture group.
Presentation:
For the 6-minute presentation, you are required to showcase all of your key points mentioned in your essay in front of your classmates to help them gain insights into your culture and acknowledge how they should communicate with you with the purpose of mutual collaboration and harmony. It help you become aware of the fact that how your cultural identity affects your communications.
Essay:
Your reflective essay conveys who you are as a member of a cultural group, what beliefs, values, practices, traditions and so forth are considered as important in your culture, and why they are important for members of your culture. Reflecting on your own culture will greatly help you to understand you and other cultures (which is the purpose of Assessment 2). Your cultural group can be defined at national level (e.g.,Vietnamese) or at sub-cultural level (e.g., ethnic groups, hobby groups). An example of a theoretical model that you may choose to use in your assignment is Dodd's model of culture. You may apply it to explain how the values within the inner core of a culture are reflected in the attitudes and behaviours of your cultural group. You need to define all key concepts used in the theories or theoretical models you have chosen to include in your essay so that you can demonstrate your understanding of knowledge and your ability to apply it.
Include the following sections in your reflective essay:
• Title of your essay.
• Brief description of the cultural group with which you identify, including values, beliefs, traditions, or customs that you choose to focus on.
• Definition and explanation of the theories and/or the conceptual model you choose to frame the discussion of your culture.
• Your reflection on what it means to you to identify as a member of your cultural group, and how your cultural identity shapes your thinking and doing.
Notes: The detailed marking criteria for this assessment will be provided to you by your lecturer. 4

### Target Audience Research Project
- Weight: 50.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- CLO: LO1, LO2, LO3, LO4, LO5, LO6, LO7
- Question Type: Option 1: Group Project Option 2: (For Constructivism Approach only): Follow lecturer's proposal
- Note: Option 1:
The mark will be given based on each student's performance, not on the whole group's. It means each student will get their own mark.
Task Description:
The assessment, including one group report and one group presentation, provides you with an opportunity to understand your future target audience by experiencing their culture/subculture as an "outsider".
As communicators, we want our audience to receive our messages and understand them as we intended them regardless of different culture identity. This project can help you to come up with some ideas to tailor your messages, visual content and pick the media channels you may use to reach your target audiences. Students will work in group of 2 or 3 in order to conduct this project.
Presentation:
For the 30-minute presentation, you are required to showcase all of your key points mentioned in your report in front of your classmates to help them gain insights into various cultures/subcultures, and all challenges they may have to face in the future regrading cross cultural conflict and misunderstanding. Your presentation should showcase how we can communicate with our target audiences to spread the right message using the right media platforms.
Report:
To accomplish this assignment, you need to:
• Conduct some serious research into a culture/subculture and the people who have belonged to this culture group
• Attend and participate (at least) one cultural event. This event should be organised by members of a culture (or subculture) other than your own. Examples of these cultural events include, but not limited to, cultural fairs, weddings, celebration of festivals, etc. You may need to search the internet, and any other sources in order to locate those cultural events.
• Formulate at least 10 questions you want to ask people from that culture about their experiences of being a member of that cultural group.
• Interview 3-5 people from this cultural group at the event. You may also observe their interactions and behaviours at the event.
• Identify a problem or a set of related problems leading to miscommunication between members of this cultural group and you (e.g., stereotypes, different value orientations, communication styles, etc.).
• Apply at least one theory (or one conceptual model) to analyse the identified intercultural problem (e.g., value orientations, high-context and low-context culture, communication accommodation, etc.).
• Reflect on your learning through this intercultural experience, suggest some methods to approach your target audiences who belong to the culture and recommend some types of appropriate content which can resonate with them and some media platforms which can reach them.
Include the following sections in your report:
• Title of the report.
• Introduction: State the culture you have chosen and provide a justification for your choice of the cultural group and the event to attend.
• The event: Provide a brief description of the event and its significance to the people who organised it.
• Observations and interactions: Explain what you observed at the event that you understood accurately and what was puzzling to you. Describe your interview questions and your interviewees (e.g., why do you choose to interview them; what are the major findings from the interviews).
• Problems, solutions, suggestions and application of theories: Identify what people think might be the major difficulties in communication between people from diffrent cultural groups. Analyse the potential problems by drawing on theories and concepts learned in the course. Provide some suggestions in order to approach the target audiences effectively and positively.
• Reflection: Reflect on your experience of interacting with people in a different cultural situation that is not as predictable as communicating in your familiar cultural environment; and discuss what you have learned from this intercultural experience
Notes: The detailed marking criteria for this assessment will be provided to you by your lecturer.
Each group will need to conduct an in-depth interview (choice of person interviewed to be approved by the instructor) and include the full transcript of the interview in the annex section of the report. It is expected that between 10 and 12 questions be raised during the interview.
