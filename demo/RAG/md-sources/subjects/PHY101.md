### Description - PHY101
The course covers a sufficient understanding of fundamental physics concepts, and helps students apply this conceptual understanding to quantitative problem solving. It also helps students to perceive underlying physics phenomena through practical lab sessions. Topic covered: Measurements, Mechanics, Thermodynamics, Electromagnetisms, Optics, Quantum Physics and Semiconductors.

### Student Tasks - PHY101

## Assessment - PHY101

### Assignment
- Weight: 20.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- Question Type: Multiple choice questions or suitable format
- Note: Instruction and shedules for Progress tests must be presented in the Course Implementation Plan approved by the director of the campus.

Instructor has responsibility to review the test for students after graded.

### Laboratory
- Weight: 20.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- Question Type: Questions at the end of lab manuals
- Note: Lab manuals should be given to students at the beginning of the course

Be aware that students will not be able to make up the lab.

If students miss a lab they must either have a written doctor’s excuse or an excuse approved by your laboratory instructor to avoid having a score of zero recorded for the missed lab.

Bring a calculator to the laboratory. The lab reports are normally collected right after the lab session.

### Project
- Weight: 20.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- Question Type: follow requirement of appropriate case studies
- Note: - Every project starts with 45 minute instruction and group division
- Students are required to share tasks equally, do the project at home and present by group in class.
- Reports are made by group and submitted right after presentation.
- Results are marked for the whole group and returned to students in approximately 4 days after submission.
- Be aware that students will not be able to make up the late submission.

### Final exam
- Weight: 40.0%
- Completion Criteria: 4
- Question Type: Multiple choice questions marked by computer
- Note: No materials may be consulted during an exam unless specifically permitted by your instructor.

A calculator is necessary for exams, but all memory must be erased.
