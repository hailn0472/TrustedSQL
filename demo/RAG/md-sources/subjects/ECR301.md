### Description - ECR301
Students develop and apply critical reading skills through the analysis of texts taken from authentic sources (journals, newspapers, magazines and websites) and a variety of academic disciplines. Students are encouraged to develop their comprehension, while at the same time forming a reasoned assessment of the effectiveness and validity of the text.

## Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) - ECR301

- **CLO1**: Use different reading strategies for different tasks, identify what critical reading is (and what it is not); understand the importance of critical reading skill; make questions and find answers as you read critically; differentiate between critical reading and other reading strategies; Develop and integrate criticality into reading practices.
- **CLO2**: Analyze the key components of a critical review; differentiate between a critical review and a research essay; structure a critical review
- **CLO3**: Evaluate whether or not a text is academic; describe the peer-review process works; evaluate why peer-reviewed texts are preferable for academic study; understand the importance of the publication date of a text
- **CLO4**: Analyze the importance of knowing something about the author of a text and the people quoted by the author; recognize and reason bias
- **CLO5**: Identify the author’s stance; how the author’s intended audience influences the text; why the author’s purpose is important
- **CLO6**: Distinguish facts from opinions
- **CLO7**: Evaluate conclusions drawn from author experience; recognize the steps of the scientific method; evaluate important information as reading reports of empirical research
- **CLO8**: Identify and evaluate evidence not based on author experience: anecdotes, visual items, statistics and quotations from others; how authors try to persuade readers using no evidence at all; how to evaluate these kinds of evidence
- **CLO9**: Analyze the importance of considering the text in its broader context; recognize a school of thought and understand the importance of knowing the schools of thought in your area of study; how to approach a text that presents an opinion or theory very different from anything else you have read
- **CLO10**: Explain why it is important to ask yourself whether the text supports your own experiences; why it can be difficult to do this
