### Description - ECO201
This course will study the economic relations among nations through analyzing the exchange of goods, services as well as the linkages between trade, labor and capital movements, international fragmentation of production, economic well-being and the income distribution and to identify and critically examine policy implications of these linkages. Besides that, the course provides students both classical and modern theories of international trade in goods and services, as well as empirical research on trade . A substantial fraction of the course is dedicated to examination of efficient trade policies. This course also covers the balance of payments, exchange rates and open-economy macroeconomics.

## Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) - ECO201

- **CLO1**: Recognize the overview of world trade, cause of trade, the pattern of word trade, the way of word trade has evolved over time
- **CLO2**: Demonstrate the global capital market: performance and policy problems
- **CLO3**: Identify the idea of optimum currency areas
- **CLO4**: Demonstrate the understanding of Growth, Crises and International Speculation
- **CLO5**: Identify the the main Traditional Models of International Trade
- **CLO6**: Understand the Imperfect Competition and New Theories of International Trade
- **CLO7**: State the linkages between trade, labor and capital movements, international fragmentation of production, economic well-being and the income distribution and to identify and critically examine policy implications of these linkages
- **CLO8**: Identify International factor mobility
- **CLO9**: Analyze instruments and consequences of trade policy measures
- **CLO10**: Discuss the political economy of Trade Policy: the motivation for protectionist policies, examples of current trade agreements, Recent controversies in trade policy and trade retaliation, Trade policy in developing countries, links between trade and the environment.
- **CLO11**: Understand the exchange Rates, Price Levels and Money
- **CLO12**: Analyze clearly the intertemporal approach to current account, balance of payments and the trade balance;
