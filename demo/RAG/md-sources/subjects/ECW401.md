### Description - ECW401
This course is designed to develop students' creative writing abilities by exploring the rich landscape of fictional literature. It focuses on the art of storytelling and guides students through the process of character development, plot structuring, setting creation, and narrative techniques through the analysis of influential works. By the end of the course, students will have the opportunity to craft their own fictional stories, incorporating elements learned from both classic and contemporary literature.

### Student Tasks - ECW401
- Students must attend more than 80% of contact sessions in order to be eligible for the final examination. - Students are responsible for completing all exercises, assignments, and labs given by the instructor in class or at home and submitting them on time. - Students use laptops in class only for learning purposes.

## Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) - ECW401

- **CLO1**: Identify and analyze key elements of fiction, including character development, plot structure, setting, and narrative techniques, as demonstrated through the study of selected literary works.
- **CLO2**: Apply understanding of literature elements to the critique of published works.
- **CLO3**: Evaluate and critique the narrative techniques used in both peer writing and professional works, providing constructive feedback that reflects an understanding of the conventions of fiction writing.
- **CLO4**: Create original fictional stories that incorporate effective use of character development, dialogue, conflict, and resolution, showcasing creativity and technical mastery.
- **CLO5**: Present and defend creative works orally and in writing, demonstrating effective communication skills and the ability to articulate the creative choices made during the writing process.

## Assessment - ECW401

### Assignments
- Weight: 30.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- CLO: CLO4
- Question Type: Assignment 1:
- Students submit a complete short story (1,500–2,000 words) that focus on specific elements of fiction, such as plot, character sketches, setting descriptions, or dialogues. Each assignment should demonstrate mastery of one or more narrative techniques.
- Students include a group process report where they briefly describe their contributions and how they navigated the collaborative process.

Assignment 2:
Students participate in peer critique sessions where they provide constructive feedback on their classmates’ drafts. They should focus on narrative techniques such as plot coherence, character development, setting, and dialogues.
- Each student submits a brief individual reflection on the feedback they provided and received during the in-class session.
- Note: Instruction and schedules for the assignments must be presented in the Course Implementation Plan approved by the director of the campus.
The instructor is responsible for reviewing the assignments with students after they have been graded.

### Participation
- Weight: 15.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- CLO: CLO1, CLO2, CLO3, CLO4, CLO5

### Progress Test
- Weight: 15.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- CLO: CLO1, CLO2, CLO3
- Question Type: Students write short (300-500 words) responses analyzing specific aspects of the selected literary works, focusing on elements like character development, plot structure.
Selected Texts:
1. Jane Eyre by Charlotte Brontë 
2. The Great Gatsby by F. Scott Fitzgerald
- Note: Instruction and schedules for the progress tests must be presented in the Course Implementation Plan approved by the director of the campus.
The instructor is responsible for reviewing the writings with students after they have been graded.

### Final exam
- Weight: 40.0%
- Completion Criteria: 4
- CLO: CLO5
- Question Type: Students present their final creative project to the class, discussing their writing process, the challenges faced, and the creative choices made during the project’s development. This is followed by a Q&A session where students defend their work against the exam board.
