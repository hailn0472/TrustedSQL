### Description - EPG301
- Course objective: The aim of the course is to enable students to critically evaluate how context impacts the interpretation of meaning in communication. Additionally, students will be equipped to apply their understanding of pragmatics in order to produce precise and contextually appropriate content in English for various communicative purposes.  - Course description: This course focuses on how context influences the interpretation of meaning in communication. The course provides understanding on contextual meaning so that students will be able to analyze how context, including cultural background and situational factors, shapes the meaning of utterances. 1. Emphasize the practical skills that students will learn in this course. 2. Observe coherence and consistency in using active voice statements. 3. The course includes an exploration of speech act concepts, illustrating how utterances can serve as requests, commands, questions, or statements based on the context in which they are used. Concepts of speech acts, including how utterances can function as requests, commands, questions, or statements, depending on context are provided with . Mastery of pragmatics equips students with the ability to navigate complex social and professional situations, fostering better negotiation, collaboration, and overall communication competence, which are invaluable assets in careers such as business, education, law, media, and international relations.  - Teaching and Learning Methodology: This course applies instructional, communicative, and interactive discussions. Instructions on pre-lesson and post lesson exercises are designed to apply learnt skills. Open minded approach to questions and any misunderstandings that may arise during lessons is preferred. Group work and pair work are encouraged to foster interaction and discussion. Group presentations are carried out to reflect students' comprehension on pragmatic aspects in the course. Linguistic data analysis may particularly be included as the students are required to do the Assignment across specific languages.

## Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs) - EPG301

- **CLO1**: Critique fundamental concepts and principles of pragmatics by examining and contrasting various viewpoints on the subject.
- **CLO2**: Assess sociocultural views on the use of language across different contexts.
- **CLO3**: Analyze ambiguity and vagueness in language leading to multiple interpretations and misunderstandings.
- **CLO4**: Explore variations of pragmatic norms and expectations in different cultures fostering a greater understanding of cross-cultural communication.
- **CLO5**: Develop critical thinking skills to evaluate how language is used in various social contexts, including media, politics, and interpersonal relationships.
- **CLO6**: Generate effective communication in academic and professional contexts with knowledge of pragmatics
- **CLO7**: Construct linguistic knowledge by evaluating online resources and establishing ethical considerations related to differences in cultures
- **CLO8**: Develop professionalism and respect with other members in class activities, discussions, and group work

## Assessment - EPG301

### Assignment
- Weight: 20.0%
- Completion Criteria: >0
- CLO: CLO6,7,8
- Question Type: Presentation
- Note: Topics for group presentation:
1. The Role of Context in Pragmatics: Exploring how different contexts shape meaning and communication structures.
2. Speech Act Theory in Everyday Communication: Analyzing the functions of speech acts and their impact on interactions.
3. Cooperative Principle and Its Applications: Investigating how the principles of cooperation guide effective communication.
4. Politeness vs. Impoliteness: A study of strategies used in varying cultural contexts to navigate politeness and impoliteness.
5. Critical Discourse Analysis and Power Dynamics: Examining how language reflects and influences power relationships in society.
6. Intercultural Pragmatics: Bridging Communication Gaps: Analyzing challenges and strategies for effective communication across cultures.
7. Implicature in Communication: Understanding how speakers imply meanings beyond the literal interpretation of their words.
8. Conversation Analysis and English as a Lingua Franca (ELF): Exploring how conversation analysis can enhance communication in multilingual settings.
9. The Impact of Culture on Indirect Communication: Investigating how cultural norms shape preferences for directness or indirectness in conversation.
10. Teaching Pragmatics in Language Education: Strategies for integrating pragmatic competence into language learning curricula.
Each presentation should be:
Informative and Analytical: provide a clear overview of the assigned topic, delve into its theoretical background, and analyze its practical applications. Emphasis should be placed on making complex ideas accessible and engaging for the audience.
Interactive: include activities, questions, or discussions to involve the audience and encourage engagement with the material.
Concise and Structured: be well-organized, with clear sections covering introduction, main content, practical examples, and a conclusion. Each section should be purposeful and connected to the topic’s key ideas.
